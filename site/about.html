The Fonds P. Nwyia and its Index

Paul Nwyia (1925–1980), Jesuit scholar of Islamic mysticism and Director of Studies at the École Pratique des Hautes Études, assembled over his career a working collection of Sufi texts — the Fonds P. Nwyia, held at the Bibliothèque Orientale of the Université Saint-Joseph in Beirut. This site is a digital edition of his Index des Lieux: the catalogue in which he recorded, manuscript by manuscript, the witnesses he consulted across the libraries of Europe and the Islamic world.

What the catalogue records

Each entry preserves Nwyia's own data — city, library, shelfmark, foliation, title and author — cleaned and rendered in readable form, with Arabic titles given alongside a DIN 31635 transliteration and, where established, an English translation. A dedicated archive (Fonds cote) field carries Nwyia's own ordering of the collection; it is not a holding-library reference, which is why works here are identified by author and title rather than by shelfmark alone.

Publication status

A principal aim of the project is to record whether each text has appeared in a modern critical edition. Where it has, the entry carries the citation and, where available, a link to the printed text. For the early Sufi authors this draws heavily on the bibliography of Digital Sufism; several texts were edited by Nwyia himself (the Ḥikam of Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh, 1972; the Trois œuvres inédites of Šaqīq al-Balḫī, Ibn ʿAṭāʾ and al-Niffarī, 1973; the letters of Ibn ʿAbbād al-Rundī).

Provenance recovered: the Tübingen Depot

Eleven manuscripts Nwyia records as held at Tübingen — all copies of Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh's al-Ḥikam al-ʿAṭāʾiyya, cited by bare number — are in fact holdings of the Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin. During the Second World War the oriental manuscripts of the Preußische Staatsbibliothek were evacuated westward and, from 1948, gathered at the Universitätsbibliothek Tübingen as the Tübinger Depot der Preußischen Staatsbibliothek, dissolved only in 1968. Nwyia, consulting them in that interval, recorded the place at which he read them. Each has been recoded to its true Berlin shelfmark (Petermann, Landberg, Sprenger, Wetzstein) and verified against the union catalogue Qalamos; all belong to the Ḥikam group catalogued by Ahlwardt under no. 8690.

Selected identification corrections

  • r0074 — Ādāb al-aqṭāb. Nwyia's “Maḥmūd al-Kāzawānī” is a misreading; the author is ʿAlī b. Aḥmad al-Ḥamawī al-Shādhilī al-Kīzwānī.
  • Vatican / Escorial mislabels. Several Vaticani arabi (the al-Qūnawī group: Vat. ar. 291, 297, 1397, 1428, 1436, 1453) and an Escorial manuscript had been mis-assigned to the BnF through a shared shelfmark prefix; now corrected.
  • BnF titles. Arabe 6630 is al-Ghazālī's Tahāfut al-falāsifa (not Tahāfut al-tahāfut); Arabe 6819 is Mullā Ṣadrā's al-Asfār al-arbaʿa.
  • Vat. ar. 1436. ff. 82a–85a is al-Qūnawī's Risālat manāzil al-abdāl (Nwyia read manāzil al-ʿibāda).

Sources & credits

Provenance research draws on Werner Schochow, Bücherschicksale (de Gruyter, 2003); the Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin Orientabteilung; Qalamos; the Bibliothèque nationale de France; the Digital Sufism bibliography of early Sufi sources; and the IdRef/Sudoc record of Nwyia's own publications. Manuscript identifications are keyed to the project's working data and remain subject to revision as further witnesses are confirmed.
